Young Galileo grew up in Pisa, Italy, where his father, a musician, didn’t just play music: he measured it. From him, Galileo learned to **test things, not just believe them**.

A famous story says he timed a swinging cathedral lamp with his own pulse. He rolled balls down long wooden ramps, again and again, measuring carefully. Then, in 1609, he built his own telescope and pointed it at the sky. The Moon had mountains and craters! The Milky Way was made of thousands of stars! And four tiny moons were travelling around Jupiter. Some powerful people didn’t like his ideas, but Galileo kept thinking and writing, and today we know he was right: the Earth travels around the Sun.

A true story about looking closely, measuring carefully and being brave enough to look again, with the refrain **Look. Measure. Look again!** Perfect for young stargazers and children who love to ask “how do you know?”

**Inside you’ll also find:** a “For grown-ups” biography and timeline, a picture glossary of words to know, four simple activities to try at home (including a Moon diary and a pendulum), and a page for your child to draw what they saw.
