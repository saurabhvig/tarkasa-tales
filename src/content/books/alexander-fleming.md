Young Alec grew up on a windy hill farm in Scotland. On his long walks to school he noticed everything: a bird’s nest, a fox’s tracks, a trout hiding in a stream, the small things other people walked straight past.

He grew up to study germs in a famously messy laboratory in London, where he even painted pictures with colourful bacteria! Then, in the summer of 1928, he came back from holiday to find a fuzzy blue-green mould on a forgotten dish, with a clear ring around it where the germs had disappeared. Instead of throwing it away, Alec wondered why. His discovery, **penicillin**, later became a medicine that saved millions of lives, thanks to a team of scientists who worked out how to make it.

A true story about curiosity, happy accidents and teamwork, with the refrain **Notice. Wonder. Find out.** Perfect for children who love magnifying glasses, nature walks and asking “what’s that?”

**Inside you’ll also find:** a “For grown-ups” biography and timeline, a picture glossary of words to know, four safe, simple activities to try at home, and a page for your child to record something they noticed.
