When Ada was twelve, she wanted to fly! She studied birds’ wings, tested paper, silk and feathers, and drew plans for a flying machine in a book she called *Flyology*.

At seventeen, she saw something amazing at a party: inventor Charles Babbage’s gleaming brass machine that could do sums all by itself. While other guests stared, Ada wanted to know **how it worked**. Years later, she wrote out, step by step, how Babbage’s planned Analytical Engine could work out a special list of numbers. Today we call instructions like that **a computer program**. Ada even imagined that one day such machines might make music!

A true story about imagination and careful thinking working together, what Ada called “poetical science”, with the refrain **Imagine it. Plan it. Step by step!** Perfect for young inventors, pattern-makers and future coders.

**Inside you’ll also find:** a “For grown-ups” biography and timeline, a picture glossary of words to know, four simple activities to try at home (including a “robot friend” instructions game), and a page for your child to design their own machine.
