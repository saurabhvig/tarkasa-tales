## Documented facts vs popular anecdotes

| Book | Documented (records, letters, own accounts, dated events) | Popular anecdote (told later, not independently recorded) |
|---|---|---|
| Marie Curie | Worked in a cold shed; named polonium after Poland; she and Bronya helped pay for each other's studies; first person to win two Nobel Prizes | — |
| Albert Einstein | The compass at about age 5 (his own written recollection); rejected job letters; the 1919 eclipse | Riding beside a light beam is his own later recollection of a thought experiment, not an event |
| Alexander Fleming | Lysozyme from his nasal drip (1921–22); the mould on a culture plate after his holiday (1928); Florey and Chain's team made penicillin a medicine | Exactly how the mould spore got onto the plate is still debated |
| Galileo Galilei | His father Vincenzo's experiments with strings; Moon mountains and Jupiter's moons (1610) | **Timing the swinging cathedral lamp** comes from a biography written after his death (Viviani) |
| Isaac Newton | The prism experiments; "standing on the shoulders of giants" (letter to Hooke, 1675) | **The falling apple** (Newton's own story, told to William Stukeley late in life; the apple hitting his head is a myth); **mouse-powered windmill** and **lantern kite mistaken for a comet** (both from Stukeley's memoir) |
| Jane Goodall | Waiting in the henhouse as a small child (her own account); David Greybeard using grass stems as tools (1960) | — |
| Katherine Johnson | John Glenn asking for "the girl" to check the numbers; "Is there a law against it?" (her own account in interviews) | — |
| Mae Jemison | "Don't you mean a nurse?" (her own account); the frog-egg experiment on STS-47 (NASA) | — |
| George Washington Carver | "Plant Doctor" nickname; walking about 10 miles to the school in Neosho; the Jesup "school on wheels" | — |
| Ada Lovelace | Flying-machine plans at 12 (her letters); long illness; her published notes with the Bernoulli-number method | "First computer programmer" is a modern interpretation, hence "often regarded as" |
| Newsletter | Hammer and feather dropped on the Moon, Apollo 15, 2 Aug 1971 (filmed) | — |

**Suggestion:** where our own new wording mentions one of the anecdotes, use "the story goes" or "he later said", as done in the Great Drop Test. The books' own texts are yours to keep. Picture books commonly tell these stories, and the "For grown-ups" pages are a good place to note them.
