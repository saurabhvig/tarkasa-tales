Isaac Newton

The Great Drop Test

One day an apple fell in Isaac’s orchard. Why does it always fall straight down? Be a scientist like Isaac and test it yourself.

Look. Ask. Test.

1. Look

Hold two things at the same height, one in each hand.

2. Ask

Which will land first? Make a guess before you drop them.

3. Test

Let go at the same time. Watch closely. Test three times!

My drop test

Circle your guess, then tick who landed first in each test (or = if they landed together).

Stay safe: drop only soft things that cannot break, from standing height. No climbing on chairs or tables. A grown-up can watch the floor and help decide which landed first.

What did you find out?

What scientists know

Things fall down because the Earth pulls them. This pull is called gravity.

A flat sheet of paper floats down slowly because the air pushes against it. Scrunch it into a ball, and it falls much faster!

Isaac wondered: could the same pull reach all the way to the Moon? He found out that it does. Gravity keeps the Moon travelling around the Earth.

My big question

Isaac asked about a falling apple. What everyday thing do you wonder about?

For grown-ups: when your child asks a question, try: “Let’s look closely, ask what might happen, and test it.” A fair test changes only one thing at a time, and repeating it three times makes the answer more trustworthy.
